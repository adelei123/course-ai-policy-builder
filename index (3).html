A TEN-MINUTE AI POLICY BUILDER

Draft a GenAI policy that fits your course.

Make a series of practical choices about how students may or may not use generative AI, what they need to disclose or document, and how your approach connects to student learning. As you go, the builder will assemble your choices into an editable syllabus statement informed by current UO guidance.

What are your course expectations for generative AI use?

UO instructors are expected to clearly explain what uses of generative AI are allowed and not allowed in their courses. The approach you choose should reflect your course learning objectives and any guidance from your department or unit.

Submitted content and disclosure · 2 decisions

What may students submit, and when should they disclose GenAI use?

Using GenAI during the work process is not the same as including AI-generated content in submitted work. Open each question below to decide what may appear in submitted work and when students should tell you they used GenAI. Choosing most answers in question 1 moves you on to question 2.

Things to consider ▾
Consider what the submitted work is meant to demonstrate. If students include AI-generated content, decide whether they must evaluate, verify, revise, or otherwise transform it—and make those expectations clear in the assignment instructions.

This includes AI-generated text, images, code, audio, video, translations, analyses, or other material incorporated into what a student submits.

Things to consider ▾
Disclosure can serve different purposes: acknowledging assistance, making students’ decision-making visible, or helping you understand how GenAI influenced their work. Choose an expectation that matches what you actually need students to communicate.

How should students acknowledge and document GenAI use?

Decide how students should acknowledge AI-generated content when it is permitted in submitted work and whether they should document how GenAI influenced their work process. Choosing most answers in question 1 moves you on to question 2.

AI-generated content is generally not permitted in submitted work under your course-level policy. If an assignment allows an exception, you may set acknowledgment or citation expectations in that assignment or add a course-level expectation below.

CHOOSING NOT TO USE GENAI — OPTIONAL

Would you like to address students who choose not to use GenAI?

Some students may have limited access to GenAI tools or may prefer not to use them. If GenAI use is optional in your course, you can clarify that students can complete the course without it.

Before you use this draft ▾

This builder is one tool for working through course-level GenAI decisions and drafting language you can adapt. It is not a substitute for reviewing current University of Oregon guidance or for using your own judgment about what fits your course and learning objectives.

Before adding this draft to your syllabus, review the UO Artificial Intelligence Resource Guide for current guidance, teaching practices, sample policies, privacy considerations, academic-integrity information, and other resources that continue to evolve. You should also check with your department, program, school, or college about any additional expectations or specific language that may apply to your course.

If you have questions about suspected academic misconduct or possible grade penalties, consult the Office of Student Conduct and Community Standards guidance before acting.

Revisit expectations during the term ▾

A syllabus statement works best when it is reinforced as students encounter actual assignments and tools. Consider revisiting your expectations after the first week and at a few relevant points during the course.

You might use:

  • a discussion prompt about what students have tried;
  • a short announcement or recorded update;
  • a reminder within assignment instructions;
  • a midterm check-in after students have applied the policy; or
  • a live conversation in a synchronous course.

Each time, reinforce three things: what GenAI use is appropriate for the work, what students remain responsible for doing, and why those expectations support the intended learning.

Keep the conversation open. Students may not raise questions about GenAI on their own. Revisit your expectations throughout the course and regularly invite questions about how they apply.
